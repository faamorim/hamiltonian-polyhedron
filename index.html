Hamiltonian Cycles on Polyhedra

A Hamiltonian cycle visits every vertex exactly once. On a polyhedron's surface it also acts as a seam: it splits the faces into two caps, and because the cycle itself passes through every vertex, both caps keep the full vertex set on their rim when pulled apart. Drag to orbit, use the panel to separate the caps.

Make it

One cut, in paper and in plastic. The sizes are a ladder rather than a slider because the hinge is a fixed thickness in millimetres while the faces scale — each rung is generated and checked at its own size.

These files are for the original cut, not the one you drew. Every STL and PDF here was generated and checked offline against the cycle this page ships with. Your cycle splits the solid on screen, but nothing has folded it, measured the overlap, or confirmed it comes apart as two printable strips — so offering you a file for it would be a guess wearing a checked file's clothes.
